What this model explains

A solid-core nuclear thermal rocket uses fission heat to heat hydrogen, then expands it through a nozzle. The solid nuclear fuel remains in the core; hydrogen is the propellant leaving the engine.

The model shows a simplified serial cooling route, a hollow pressure vessel and reflector, a representative fuel bundle with open passages, and a hollow nozzle with cooling tubes. Longitudinal inspection removes foreground fuel elements as well as casing sectors so the interior remains readable.

Geometry, fuel-element count, channel count, proportions, colors and tracer speed are illustrative. The turbine-drive branches, control drums, shielding and detailed manifolds are omitted. This is an educational visualization, not a reactor design or a thermal-fluid simulation.
